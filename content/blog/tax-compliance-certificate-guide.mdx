## What Is a Tax Compliance Certificate?

A Tax Compliance Certificate (TCC) is an official document issued by the Kenya Revenue Authority (KRA) that confirms a taxpayer has met their tax obligations. It serves as proof of good standing with KRA.

## When Do You Need a TCC?

A TCC is commonly required for:

- **Government tenders** — Most public procurement processes require a valid TCC
- **Professional licensing** — Renewal of professional and business licences
- **Regulatory approvals** — Various regulatory bodies require proof of tax compliance
- **Business partnerships** — Some organizations require a TCC before entering into contracts
- **Import and export activities** — Customs processes may require a TCC

## Requirements for a TCC

To obtain a TCC, you must demonstrate compliance with your tax obligations:

1. **Valid KRA PIN** — Both individual and business PINs as applicable
2. **All tax returns filed** — Income tax, VAT, PAYE, and withholding tax returns must be current
3. **No outstanding tax debts** — All assessed taxes must be paid or have an approved payment arrangement
4. **Updated KRA records** — Contact information and business details must be current on iTax

## Application Process

### Step 1: Compliance Review

Before applying, review your iTax account to ensure all returns are filed and payments are up to date. This is the most important step.

### Step 2: Address Outstanding Issues

If there are unfiled returns or pending assessments, these must be resolved before the application. Swift Doc can assist with filing outstanding returns.

### Step 3: Submit Application on iTax

Log into your iTax account and submit the TCC application. The system will run an automated compliance check.

### Step 4: KRA Processing

KRA reviews the application against your compliance history. If everything is in order, the certificate is issued electronically.

### Step 5: Download Your TCC

Upon approval, download the TCC from your iTax account. Verify the details and validity period.

## Common Reasons for TCC Rejection

- Unfiled tax returns
- Outstanding tax assessments or penalties
- Incomplete or incorrect taxpayer information
- Unresolved tax disputes
